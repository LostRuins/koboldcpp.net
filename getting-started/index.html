Your first local AI, step by step

Let’s get your first model running.

A short path from a fresh download to your first conversation. No command-line experience required for the desktop launcher.

You need two things: the KoboldCpp app and a compatible AI model. A quick launch template can download the model files for you. You do not need a KoboldCpp account or an API key for local generation.

On this page1. Get the app2. Choose a model or template3. Check your hardware settings4. Launch and say hello5. Try something new

1. Get the app

Open our download guide and follow the official GitHub release link for your operating system. Keep the executable in a folder you can find again.

Leave space for models: they can be several gigabytes each. Download the app first, then decide how you want to get your model.

2. Choose a model or template

The guided route: use a template

Open KoboldCpp’s launcher and use Help to browse Newbie Templates, or visit the newbie template collection. Download a .kcppt file, load it in the launcher, review its settings, and launch. Templates can fetch multiple model files, so allow time and disk space for the initial downloads.

The manual route: use a GGUF

Download a compatible .gguf text model, then select it in the launcher’s model field. For a first experiment, a smaller model is easier to fit than a large one. Our model guide explains sizes, quantization, and memory.

Choose one quantization variant of a model. If that variant is split into numbered shards, download all of its matching shards. You do not need every alternative quantization in the repository.

3. Check your hardware settings

Start with the launcher’s suggested settings. The important choices are the backend/preset, GPU layers, and context size.

HardwareStarting point
NVIDIA GPUCUDA with a compatible build; Vulkan is another option.
AMD or Intel GPUVulkan, with a compatible GPU and driver.
Apple SiliconMetal GPU acceleration with a supported build.
CPU onlyCPU mode and a small model. Generation may be slower.

GPU layers move work into video memory. Recent builds support automatic fitting with --autofit or --gpulayers -1; manual settings remain useful when you need more control. Avoid combining autofit with manual tensor overrides.

Context is the amount of text the model can work with at once. Start around 2,048–4,096 tokens, within the model’s supported context, and increase only when there is enough memory. Very large context settings can make an otherwise manageable model run out of memory.

4. Launch and say hello

  1. Click Launch and wait for loading or downloads to finish.
  2. Keep the KoboldCpp process running. If a browser does not open automatically, visit http://localhost:5001 on the same computer.
  3. In KoboldAI Lite, choose Instruct for an assistant-style task or Chat for a conversation.
  4. Try a short request, such as “Give me three ideas for a cozy mystery set in a bookshop.”
  5. Use Save / Load to export a conversation or story you want to keep.

The browser is your interface; the running KoboldCpp process does the model work. Closing that process stops local generation.

5. Try something new

For fiction, explore Story mode, memory, world info, and character cards. For image, voice, or music features, load the corresponding models or templates first. A text model by itself does not enable every media feature.

The hosted KoboldAI Lite web UI is another way to use the interface with a supported provider. Your local copy is already bundled with KoboldCpp. When you choose a remote provider, your prompts go to that provider.
